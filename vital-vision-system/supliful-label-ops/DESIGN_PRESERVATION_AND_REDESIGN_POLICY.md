**Status:** Active

## Purpose

This policy defines how Vital Vision Shop approaches existing label designs when planning improvements, redesigns, or new labels. It prevents unnecessary redesigns that disrupt brand recognition while encouraging thoughtful, strategic elevation of the label quality.

---

## Core Principle

**Preserve what works. Improve what is weak. Redesign only with strategic intent.**

Existing Vital Vision labels carry brand equity. Customers who have already purchased Inner Calm or Inner Bloom recognize the packaging. Arbitrary redesigns reduce that recognition without improving the brand.

At the same time, labels that are unclear, crowded, non-compliant, or visually inconsistent undermine the premium brand Vital Vision is building. Those must be improved.

The test for any change is: **does this change make the label more trustworthy, more premium, and more compliant — or does it just make it different?**

---

## What to Preserve

These elements should be preserved unless there is a specific, documented reason to change them:

### 1. Brand Recognition Elements

- The "Vital Vision" brand name and its general placement
- The product naming convention ("Inner ____")
- The overall warmth and wellness feel of the label
- Any visual element that customers already associate with the brand

### 2. Core Product Identity

- The exact product name (Inner Bloom / Inner Calm / Inner Balance / Inner Grow)
- The exact subtitle (Advanced Probiotic Formula / Magnesium Glycinate / Complete Multivitamin / Hair, Skin & Nails Support)
- The core benefit claims (if they are already compliant and effective)

### 3. Elements That Already Work

Any element that scores 4 or 5 in the Premium Readiness Scorecard for its dimension should be preserved unless it conflicts with a higher-priority requirement (compliance, technical constraint, or Master Label Standard).

## What to Improve

These categories are eligible for improvement without a full redesign approval:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Readability issues | Text too small; poor contrast; crowded back panel |
| Compliance issues | Any forbidden language; missing or incorrect FDA disclaimer |
| Technical issues | Content outside safe area; matrix code zone obscured |
| Consistency gaps | Font or color differs from other Inner products unnecessarily |
| Layout inefficiencies | Elements stacked without clear zones; uneven visual weight |
| Premium feel deficiencies | Logo too small; product name not dominant; generic design elements |

**Process for targeted improvements:**
1. Document the specific issue and proposed improvement.
2. Submit to Premium Label QA Reviewer for a focused review.
3. Get Lucy's approval on the specific change.
4. Apply the change in Supliful.
5. Run the full save approval process (Workflow 03).

---

## What Requires Full Redesign Approval

A "full redesign" means significantly changing the visual identity of a label — not just fixing problems. Full redesign requires:

1. A completed design analysis (Workflow 08) showing specific deficiencies that justify the redesign.
2. A clear redesign rationale documented in `templates/premium-label-design-brief-template.md`.
3. A complete new design package created through Workflow 09.
4. Lucy's explicit approval of the design package before any build begins.
5. The full build and QA process (Workflows 09, 12, and then Workflow 01/QA → Workflow 03).

### What Constitutes a Full Redesign

- Changing the primary background color or palette dramatically
- Changing the primary font family
- Restructuring the front panel layout (not just adjusting position — fundamentally reordering zones)
- Removing the product name from its established position
- Changing the product-line differentiation color system

## Redesign Authority

| Action | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Targeted improvement (fix a specific problem) | Lucy, after agent review |
| Typography adjustment within the same family | Lucy, after agent review |
| Full front panel redesign | Lucy, after Workflow 08 analysis |
| Full label redesign (all panels) | Lucy, after Workflow 08 and explicit redesign brief |
| New product label (from scratch) | Lucy, after completing Workflow 09 brief |
| Master Label Standard update | Lucy, after Workflow 10 |

No agent, assistant, or automated system may authorize a redesign. All redesign decisions require Lucy's explicit approval documented in writing.

---

## Anti-Randomness Rule

**Labels must never be redesigned randomly or because "it seemed like a good idea."**

Every change to a Vital Vision label must be:
- Traced to a specific documented problem (compliance issue, readability failure, consistency gap, premium standard deficiency)
- OR traced to a specific strategic direction (launching a new product, adopting the Master Label Standard, aligning with a rebrand)

If a proposed change cannot be traced to one of these sources, it should not be made.

---

## Safe First, Design Second

No design change, no matter how visually appealing, may:

- Introduce forbidden language or remove required compliance elements
- Place content outside the Supliful safe area
- Obscure the matrix code zone
- Remove or improperly modify the FDA disclaimer
- Remove the Supplement Facts panel
- Be saved in Supliful without the full approval process (Workflow 03)

Design serves compliance — not the other way around.

---

## The Final Save Gate

No matter how beautiful the label is, no matter how well it scores on the Premium Readiness Scorecard:

**The final "Save label" action in Supliful always requires Lucy's explicit human approval.**

This is a permanent, non-negotiable requirement. It cannot be automated, delegated, or bypassed.

See: `checklists/save-label-approval-checklist.md` and `workflows/03-supliful-label-save-approval.md`.

| Design serves compliance | No design decision overrides compliance requirements |
