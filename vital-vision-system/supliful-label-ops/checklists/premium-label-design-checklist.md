# Premium Label Design Checklist

Use this checklist when evaluating a new or redesigned Vital Vision label for premium quality standards.
Complete this while looking at the label on screen or in print preview.

**Product:** ___________________________
**Label version:** ___________________________
**Date:** ___________________________

---

## Section 1 — First Impression (3-Second Shelf Test)

Look at the label for three seconds from normal reading distance. Then answer:

- [ ] The product name is immediately readable — no need to squint or look closely
- [ ] The brand identity is clear — this looks like a Vital Vision product
- [ ] The label looks premium — I would pay a quality price for this product
- [ ] The color palette feels intentional and cohesive — not random
- [ ] There is visual breathing room — the label does not feel crowded

**First impression score (1–5):** ___

---

## Section 2 — Typography Quality

- [ ] Product name uses the correct approved font and weight
- [ ] Product name is the visually dominant text element on the front panel
- [ ] Subtitle is clearly secondary — smaller, lighter, or different weight than product name
- [ ] Benefit statements are legible at a glance
- [ ] All body text (Supplement Facts, Suggested Use, etc.) is readable without a magnifying glass
- [ ] Legal text (FDA disclaimer, address) is present and meets minimum legibility
- [ ] No more than two font families are used (one primary, one supporting) — or a single family with weight variation
- [ ] Letter-spacing and line-height feel intentional and premium (not default/generic)

**Typography score (1–5):** ___

---

## Section 3 — Color and Visual Identity

- [ ] Primary brand color is used consistently across front and back panels
- [ ] Product-line accent color (if used) is appropriate for this product
- [ ] Text color has sufficient contrast against the background — no text disappears into the background
- [ ] The color palette feels like a premium wellness brand (not pharmaceutical, not mass-market)
- [ ] Background color or texture feels intentional — clean, elevated, on-brand
- [ ] No color is used that conflicts with the approved Vital Vision color system

**Color score (1–5):** ___

---

## Section 4 — Layout and Visual Hierarchy

- [ ] There is a clear visual hierarchy: product name → subtitle → benefits → details → legal
- [ ] The logo anchors the design (it is present, correctly sized, and not overwhelmed by other elements)
- [ ] The front panel has a natural flow — the eye moves from top to bottom without confusion
- [ ] The back panel is organized into clear sections — not a wall of text
- [ ] The Supplement Facts table is visually distinct from the surrounding copy
- [ ] There is intentional spacing between sections (they do not blend together)
- [ ] No element appears accidentally dominant or accidentally hidden

**Layout score (1–5):** ___

---

## Section 5 — Brand Consistency

- [ ] This label could be placed next to the other Vital Vision products and be recognized as the same family
- [ ] The product name matches the approved name exactly
- [ ] The subtitle matches the approved subtitle exactly
- [ ] The benefit statements use approved, on-brand language
- [ ] The overall tone is warm, premium, and wellness-focused (not clinical, aggressive, or generic)

**Brand consistency score (1–5):** ___

---

## Section 6 — Supliful Technical Readiness

- [ ] All text and logos are inside the safe area (nothing at risk of being cut)
- [ ] Background extends to the bleed edge (no white gaps)
- [ ] Matrix code zone is clear and unobstructed
- [ ] Supplement Facts table is complete and readable at print size
- [ ] Label can be reproduced in Supliful (or via Canva-to-Supliful) without workarounds

**Technical readiness score (1–5):** ___
